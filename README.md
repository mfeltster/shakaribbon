# Shaka Ribbon

Cancer awareness stickers by Fizzle. Live at **[shakaribbon.com](https://shakaribbon.com)**.

The shaka is how my people say *I see you.* The ribbon is for everyone in it — the ones who've been through it, and the ones who witnessed it. Put them together and you get the whole story.

If you count the rays in the burst, one is missing. It stays missing, **for the ones we lost.**

## Who's making this

Fizzle.

VFX artist in a past life — compositor at Method Studios, *Titanic*, *The Ring*, Super Bowl spots. A detached retina ended that in 2006. Metal caster, writer, and 2X cancer survivor in this one. Prostate in 2023. Tongue in 2024, with a radiation mask bolted over my face from July to September.

I make stickers and bronze medallions for the people who show up, and I'm probably the guy handing you one in a dealership parking lot.

## Why there's an AI in the commit history

Because there is, and I'd rather say it than have you find it.

Nobody tells you that rebuilding your life after cancer looks like this. Not the surfing. Not the meditation. Not the quiet walks with your dog at sunset that Instagram would have you believe is the whole story.

For me it looked like voice memos. Talking to myself in the car, in the shed, in the dark at 3am when the cancer thoughts got loud. Transcribing them. Filing them. Building folders inside folders inside folders like I was constructing a cathedral out of anxiety and obsessive precision — which, if you know me, is basically my love language.

That became a vault. The vault got a name — SoulForge, of course it did — and then it got a system around it, and eventually the system got good enough to build things like this website.

Somewhere in the middle of that I saw Andrej Karpathy post about using LLMs to build personal knowledge bases in Obsidian. A founding member of OpenAI, describing my exact workflow like he'd just invented it.

Except he's an engineer. He wrote scripts.

I did it by talking to an AI like it was my collaborator, my editor, my 3am thinking partner. No code. No computer science degree. Just a guy with a detached retina, two cancelled careers, two cancers, seven years of jungle ceremonies, and an absolutely unhinged commitment to not losing the thread of his own story.

Same destination. Different vehicle.

The only reason I could get there by talking is that I spent twenty years inside machines already — compositing, node graphs, debugging something at 3am until it gave up. I knew how to describe a system precisely. I just didn't know how to type one.

## What's actually in here

One self-contained HTML file. Art embedded, no build step, no framework, no dependencies. It's hosted free on GitHub Pages and costs about thirty dollars a year in domains.

The art is mine — drawn in Affinity, printed as die-cut vinyl. The words are mine. The story is definitely mine.

The commits say an AI helped, because one did.

---

## Where the money goes

Straight to the medical debt that surviving twice left behind. Two cancers cost more than anyone tells you. When that's paid down, a cut starts going to the fight at large. Until then, every sticker helps this particular survivor stay ahead of it.

That's the whole books.
